Scope: the seven areas in `tools/audit/e-research.md`. Machine: whitebook (MacBookPro11,1, i5-4278U 2c/4t, 8 GB, Arch, linux-lts 6.18.52, X11, i3 4.25.1, xcompmgr, xorg-server 21.1.24 + xf86-video-intel SNA/TearFree).
Read-only audit: nothing was changed, restarted or installed. Local numbers were read from `/proc`, `/sys` and cgroups at 03:30-03:40 on 15.10 (uptime about 1 h 37 min).

**How to read the confidence column**
- **verified**: measured or read on this machine, or read directly in a primary source I opened (source file, release notes, man page).
- **inferred**: derived from sources or local facts, not tried.
- **snippet**: only seen in a web-search summary, I could not open the page. Treat as a lead.

**Limits of the research tooling.** The search tool returns thin summaries, ArchWiki, Phoronix, systemd.io and bugzilla.kernel.org block `WebFetch`. I read ArchWiki through its `action=raw` wikitext, GitHub and freedesktop GitLab through their APIs, the man pages from the local `man`. No source I found gives a 2024-2026 benchmark of any bar or WM, so I give no RAM/CPU numbers for other projects, only for our own pieces.

### 1.2 Is staying on i3 still the best base? Yes.

- Among the floating-capable candidates I checked, i3 is the only one that is both actively maintained **and** gives per-output workspaces + a scriptable IPC, which are the two things deskd's whole design hangs on (verified from `ARCHITECTURE.md`; per-monitor workspaces absent from xfwm4's docs, inferred; I did not check every WM's multi-monitor model).
- Everything else is either a full-DE window manager (xfwm4/KWin, would re-create the work we did, with unmeasured RAM/CPU), or a classic floating WM with no better Windows behaviour than what we have already.
- The platform risk is not i3 but X11 itself: GNOME 50 deleted its X11 session ([Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/GNOME-50-Alpha)), Plasma 6.8 drops it, but the X server keeps shipping: **xorg-server 21.1.25 (2026-10-07) and a 26.1 release candidate 26.0.99.903 (2026-10-06)** are tagged ([tags](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/xorg/xserver/-/tags)); the XLibre fork exists ([Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/XLibre-25.0-Released)) and is not needed. Horizon for this laptop: the 6.18 LTS kernel is supported until Dec 2028 ([kernel.org](https://www.kernel.org/category/releases.html)).

## 3. Compositing on old Intel

### 3.1 What exists

- **picom 13** released 2026-02-07 (`v13-rc1` 2025-12-09): new backend API, `--legacy-backends` removed, `glx-no-stencil`/`resize-damage` deprecated, backends are now `xrender`, `glx`, `egl` ([release notes](https://github.com/yshui/picom/releases/tag/v13-rc1), [sample config](https://github.com/yshui/picom/blob/next/picom.sample.conf)). Forks: [pijulius/picom](https://github.com/pijulius/picom) (animations, pushed 2026-09-22); the jonaburg fork has been idle since 2024-08. The "ftlabs" repo moved and is now a 2024 fork (`r0-zero/picom`, last push 2024-07).
- **xcompmgr 1.1.10** released 2024-11-12, commits as late as 2026-05 ([tags](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/xorg/app/xcompmgr/-/tags)).
- **fastcompmgr** (xcompmgr/compton lineage): the author's CPU table in [i3 discussion 6039](https://github.com/i3/i3/discussions/6039) (move/resize/scroll): fastcompmgr 6.7/4.4/1.5 %, **xcompmgr 7.8/4.9/1.6 %, compton 26.4/6.8/17.1 %, picom 29.3/8.1/23.1 %**; no tearing fix, a bug with `xfce4-screenshooter` reported and "should have been fixed" by 2026-01-03.
- **xf86-video-intel**: still builds on Arch, but upstream's last commits are SNA video-sprite fixes from 2025-03-21 ([commits](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/xorg/driver/xf86-video-intel/-/commits/master)). ArchWiki says several distros and Mozilla/KDE recommend `modesetting` instead ([ArchWiki Intel graphics](https://wiki.archlinux.org/title/Intel_graphics), note at the top; read as raw wikitext). The modesetting TearFree merge request was **merged 2022-12-20** ([MR 1006](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/xorg/xserver/-/merge_requests/1006)) and the 26.0.99.903 tag contains TearFree code in `drmmode_display.c` (I counted 15 mentions): it ships in the coming xorg-server 26.1, not in 21.1.x.

### 3.2 How this compares with our choice

Our own bench (`CHANGELOG` round 81, `tools/compositor-bench`): no compositor 21 % of a core, xcompmgr 29-36 %, picom glx 57-61 %, picom xrender 80 %, picom with our config 88 % (browser window repainting, **glamor, before the SNA switch**). The independent table above agrees with the ordering: xcompmgr is about 4x cheaper than picom. So the xcompmgr decision stands on outside evidence too.

## 4. MacBookPro11,1 on Linux, 2025-2026

Sources for this area are thin: the ArchWiki's model table lists MacBookPro11,2 as working and verified 2025-05-06 with `broadcom-wl` ([Laptop/Apple](https://wiki.archlinux.org/title/Laptop/Apple)); the mbpfan wiki lists 11,1 as tested (kernels 3.14, 3.15, 5.4) with no notes ([Tested Mac Models](https://github.com/linux-on-mac/mbpfan/wiki/Tested-Mac-Models)). Everything below comes from model-agnostic pages and local facts.

| # | kind | finding / idea | evidence | action | benefit / effort / risk | sure? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E4.1 | perf (power) | **The Thunderbolt NHI stays powered.** `07:00.0` DSL5520 Thunderbolt 2 NHI is `runtime_status: active`, as are the upstream bridges `05:00.0` and `06:00.0` (the four downstream ports are suspended); the `thunderbolt` module is loaded. ArchWiki: "Linux still has poor power management for Thunderbolt adapters on Macs... drawing ca. 2 W additional power even when not in use"; fixes: blacklist the module and `echo auto > .../power/control`, or `acpi_osi=!Darwin` (side effects) ([Mac/Troubleshooting](https://wiki.archlinux.org/title/Mac/Troubleshooting), "Disabling Thunderbolt") | `lspci`, `/sys/bus/pci/devices/0000:07:00.0/power/*`, `lsmod`; the 2 W figure is from a different Mac model | **measure first, on battery**: read `BAT0/current_now × voltage_now` idle, `modprobe -r thunderbolt`, check that `07:00.0` goes `suspended`, read again; then blacklist. Skip `acpi_osi` (it can break backlight/ALS). The HDMI port is native, so Thunderbolt is not needed for the second screen; mini-DP-over-Thunderbolt output could be affected (untested) | up to about 1-2 W (10-25 % of idle draw if the ArchWiki number carries over) / S / low-medium (mini-DP) | verified (state), inferred (saving) |
| E4.2 | risk | **Wi-Fi: `wl` is the only driver for BCM4360 and it is declared unmaintained.** brcmfmac does not list BCM4360 (14e4:43a0) ([brcm80211 supported chips](https://wireless.docs.kernel.org/en/latest/en/users/drivers/brcm80211.html): only 4350/4356/4358/43602/4366); `b43`/`bcma` are blacklisted. ### 5.2 Findings and ideas

| # | kind | finding / idea | evidence | action | benefit / effort / risk | sure? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E5.1 | risk | **No guard against swap thrash.** With a 16 GB swapfile, "free swap below 10 %" (earlyoom's default trigger, [README](https://github.com/rfjakob/earlyoom)) fires only when 14 GB of swap is used, long after the desktop froze; earlyoom 1.9.0 (2025-09-16) only kills when *both* available memory and free swap are low. `systemd-oomd` SIGKILLs *every process in a cgroup* ([systemd-oomd(8)](https://www.freedesktop.org/software/systemd/man/latest/systemd-oomd.service.html), local man page) i.e. a whole `firefox.scope` or an entire kitty scope with a running agent | man pages, earlyoom README | **use the kernel's own valve**: the multi-gen LRU documents `min_ttl_ms` as the thrashing guard for "laptop and desktop users who don't run oomd": `echo 1000 > /sys/kernel/mm/lru_gen/min_ttl_ms` protects the last second of working set and triggers the OOM killer when it cannot fit; "N=1000 usually eliminates intolerable janks"; 3000 risks premature kills ([kernel docs](https://docs.kernel.org/admin-guide/mm/multigen_lru.html)). Persist with a `tmpfiles.d` line (`w /sys/kernel/mm/lru_gen/min_ttl_ms - - - - 1000`). Trial it with `systemd-run --scope -p MemoryMax=...` hog first. `nohang` (v0.3.0 2025-12-20, PSI-aware, [repo](https://github.com/hakavlad/nohang)) is the userspace option if a kill-one-process-with-warning policy is wanted | removes the "everything freezes for minutes" mode / XS / medium: the kernel picks the biggest `oom_score`, which today may be a claude/codex process | verified (docs, state), inferred (that it prevents freezes here: no freeze reproduced) |
| E5.2 | idea | Two-tier swap, revisited. The old reason for `zswap=0` (Firefox kept at 2 GB pushed out pages that were pulled straight back) is what the newer **zswap shrinker** targets (writes cold pages out of the compressed pool; per-memcg LRU, [LWN 950463](https://lwn.net/Articles/950463/), [LWN 944292](https://lwn.net/Articles/944292), snippets). The shrinker parameter exists here (`/sys/module/zswap/parameters/shrinker_enabled`) and the Arch kernel defaults it on. ArchWiki's zram page: with zram and fast swap devices swappiness above 100 and `page-cluster=0` are sensible ([Zram](https://wiki.archlinux.org/title/Zram)). The practical argument now is **SSD wear**: 3.7 GB written to a 2013 SSD in under two hours | `cat /sys/module/zswap/parameters/*`, `/proc/config.gz`, `/proc/vmstat` | not now: the owner chose against it, with a measurement. If the swap write rate stays high on a normal day (not an audit day), A/B once with `zswap.enabled=1` + shrinker on (a single boot-entry change in `lab`) and compare `pswpout` and Firefox tab-reload feel | less SSD writes, faster swap-in / S (one boot-entry line) / low, but it reverses an owner decision | inferred |
| E5.3 | collision | **The heaviest memory and CPU users are placed as equals of the whole desktop.** kitty creates one scope per terminal child; all are siblings of `app.slice` under `user@1000.service`, each with `cpu.weight=100`. `app.slice` (Firefox, deskd, osd, netqd, sysqd, sky-stars) therefore gets the same share as *each* agent scope under contention. See Top 10 #1 | listing of `cpu.weight` per cgroup; `man systemd.resource-control` ("split up among all units within one slice relative to their CPU time weight") | see Top 10 #1 | high / S / low | verified |
| E5.4 | note | Daemons are not the problem: all session daemons are about 1 MB PSS; a Python daemon here is 4-6 MB PSS | measured above | no work on gvfs/at-spi/portal trimming | none | verified |
| E5.5 | note | `profile-sync-daemon` (Firefox profile into tmpfs) trades RAM for SSD writes: wrong direction on an 8 GB machine. `prelockd`: last release 2020 | [prelockd](https://github.com/hakavlad/prelockd) | skip | none | verified (repo state) |

## What I did not cover

- **No benchmark was run of other WMs, bars or compositors** (read-only audit, nothing installed). Every RAM/CPU number for alttab, polybar, eww, i3status-rust, xfwm4, IceWM and picom would have to be measured on this laptop; I found no 2024-2026 source with figures for them, only the xcompmgr/picom CPU table in the fastcompmgr thread.
- **The 80 ms Python figure** was taken on a loaded machine (other audit agents, Power-saver profile, 60 % frequency cap): use it as an upper bound; the order of magnitude (interpreter start dominates, `cat` is about 20-40x cheaper) is solid.
- **`panel-telegram` and `xdotool` were not executed** (they touch display :0); the E2.1 saving is computed from the interpreter cost alone, so it is a lower bound.
- **Not opened** (blocked or paywalled): Phoronix articles, systemd.io, bugzilla.kernel.org (including the MacBookPro11,1 GPE bug 88171 and the XHC1 bug 206143), the ArchWiki human pages (read as raw wikitext instead). Claims that rest only on search summaries are marked "snippet".
- **Not covered**: Firefox beyond memory settings, Wayland alternatives (X11 is required by the current setup and nothing found changes that this year), the Xfce 4.22 plan, `facetimehd` firmware noise, GPU `i915` parameters that need root to read, battery replacement sourcing, and the aibox/tablet projects.
